01

What we found

From your brief and your public site, the risk is not that the AI models are wrong. The risk is that all four models read the same incomplete data and agree. Agreement then looks like proof. These are the three gaps I would check before trusting any recommendation.

A qualified lead is not one unit of value

High impact

Online Nikah starts at $695. A customized UAE wedding starts at $8,100. If Google Ads bids on qualified leads as equal conversions, Smart Bidding will drift toward the cheapest, fastest package. Budget and bidding decisions have to be judged on CRM revenue per package, net of refunds, since your site offers a refund if the marriage cannot go ahead.

Conversion lag will make good campaigns look bad

High impact

Court and religious weddings depend on documents, attestations and court dates. A sale can close weeks after the click. Any recommendation built on the last 7 or 14 days will undercount the campaigns that bring the high value couples, and the models will suggest cutting them.

What the platform sees

  • Lead form fills on day 0
  • Conversions counted by click date
  • Recent days look weak because sales have not closed yet
  • Same weight for every lead

What the decision should see

  • Qualified lead and sale stages from Zoho
  • A lag curve per package from BigQuery
  • Only mature cohorts used for judgement
  • Revenue value per sale, net of refunds

The pipeline needs a data gate before stage 3

Medium impact

Hypotheses should only be written once the data behind them passes a completeness check: GCLID capture rate in Zoho, offline import status, CRM stage definitions unchanged, and no tracking changes in the window. If the gate fails, the answer is "Not yet", whatever the models say.

1
Week 1

Audit the evidence chain from click to revenue

Check GCLID capture on Zoho leads, offline conversion import status and errors, which conversion actions are primary, and whether CRM stage definitions changed over time. Build a lag curve per package from BigQuery.

Why: a verdict is only as good as the data under it.

2
Weeks 2 to 4

Review each recommendation: Yes, No or Not yet

For every proposed change on budget, bidding, keywords, geo, schedule or ads, write what must be true, how it was checked, and what would change the decision.

Why: your team gets reasoning it can audit, not just an answer.

3
Week 3

Add the signals the models are missing

Revenue per package net of refunds, lag adjusted conversions, auction insights, wedding season patterns by market, landing page per package, and tracking change dates.

Why: these are the facts that most often overturn a platform-only read.

4
Weeks 3 to 4

Turn verdicts into written rules

Each verdict becomes an if/then rule with a threshold, for example: no budget increase unless the mature cohort meets the revenue target and impression share lost to budget is material.

Why: the system learns from decisions instead of repeating them.

5
Week 4

Set a rule for model disagreement

When ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and DeepSeek split, the tie breaker is the data, not the vote. Disagreement flags the claim for a direct check in BigQuery before any change.

Why: four models agreeing on the same gap is still one gap.
