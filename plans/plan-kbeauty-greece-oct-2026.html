What we found

This is built only from the numbers you published. The account itself has not been opened yet, so every point below is a hypothesis with a named check attached. Even so, the pattern is clear: the restructure moved spend from high intent Shopping traffic to cheap broad traffic, and the bidding signal was already pointed at the wrong target before that happened.

High impact

1. Performance Max took the auctions Standard Shopping used to win

Standard Shopping now underspends its budget. That means it is losing auctions it used to win on the same products. A new PMax campaign with broad audience signals and a relaxed tROAS bids more freely on the same feed, and its extra reach goes to Display, YouTube, Discover and app placements. With 90% of your traffic on mobile, app inventory is the first place to check for the +128% clicks.

Check first: Shopify orders by channel for the last 14 days. If direct, organic and email orders also fell to zero, the fault is in checkout or the site, not ads. Then a full mobile test order on COD and on card.

High impact

2. Your break-even numbers disagree, and tROAS is set from one of them

At a €38 AOV, a €7.43 CPA ceiling means a break-even ROAS of 5.11x, not 3.77x. A 3.77x target allows about €10.08 per order. The account has been running at 2.3x to 2.5x, which is roughly €15.20 to €16.52 per order. Before anyone sets a tROAS, we need to agree on one number.

Medium impact, compounding

3. Google learns from orders that never get paid

The purchase tag fires when the order is placed, so a refused COD parcel counts the same as a paid order. About 1 in 12 of your orders is refused at the door, and the courier fee, return cost and wasted stock never reach Google. Relaxing tROAS on top of that told the algorithm to chase even more of this traffic.

Signal now

  • Full €38 value on every order
  • Refused orders stay counted
  • Bidding rewards low intent COD buyers

Signal after fix

  • Value = expected profit per order
  • Refused orders retracted through conversion adjustments
  • Bidding rewards orders that are collected

1
Hours 0 to 2

Stop the burn without switching everything off

  • Confirm ads vs site: orders by channel in Shopify, plus a live mobile test order on COD and on card.
  • Use change history to put Standard Shopping budgets and bids back to their pre-restructure levels.
  • Exclude your top 25 to 30 sellers from PMax so Standard Shopping gets those auctions back. Cut PMax budget to a holding level.
  • Add account-level placement exclusions for mobile apps and junk placements, and negatives for the worst search terms in the last 14 days.
Why: this brings back the traffic that was producing orders two weeks ago, and keeps PMax data in the account instead of deleting it.
2
Day 1 to 3

Written diagnosis, backed by account data

  • Conversion goals: purchase as the only primary action, no duplicate GA4 import, no micro conversions in bidding.
  • PMax channel and placement report, search term insights, auction insights for Shopping before and after the change.
  • Merchant Center: fix disapprovals on the top sellers first, then the rest of the 554. Find out why the feed has 1,543 items against about 600 active SKUs.
Why: you get a document that names the cause from the data, not a sixth opinion.
3
Week 1 to 2

Fix the cash on delivery signal

  • Send order value as expected profit, with COD orders discounted for the refusal rate.
  • Turn on enhanced conversions with order ID, then upload retractions for refused parcels from the courier report.
  • Off the ads side: SMS or call confirmation on COD orders before dispatch, and a small incentive to prepay by card.
Why: once refused orders stop counting as wins, the algorithm stops buying more of them.
4
Week 1 to 3

Rebuild Shopping around what sells

  • Standard Shopping for the hero SKUs, split by margin. Targets come from the corrected break-even number.
  • PMax limited to the remaining catalogue, or paused, until the signal is clean.
  • Brand search protected and kept separate so it doesn't flatter the blended numbers.
Why: spend follows products that already convert, at a target the business can afford.
5
Week 3 to 4

Work on the bigger levers: checkout and second orders

  • Checkout: show shipping and COD fees before the last step, offer locker delivery, set up abandoned checkout email and SMS. 263 of 468 checkouts are not completing.
  • Retention: post-purchase and replenishment flows timed to product use, so the 8% repeat rate starts moving.
  • Weekly report on agreed KPIs: orders, Google CPA, collected revenue, refusal rate, blended ROAS against break-even.
Why: at today's margin, the traffic you already pay for gets profitable through these, not through more ad spend.

Straight answer on your two week target

5 orders a day: realistic if the drop came from the restructure, because the account was doing 6 to 7 before it.

Google CPA at or below €7.43 on every Google order within two weeks: not realistic. That needs about 5.1x ROAS, and the account has never run above 2.5x. What is realistic: brand plus the best sellers near the ceiling, the rest cut back, and a lower total spend until checkout, COD and repeat purchases move. If the data says spend less in month one, that will be the recommendation.
