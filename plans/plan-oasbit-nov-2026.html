01

What we found

Oasbit sells a clear promise: research, foundation, launch by day 30, scale through day 60, with bidding tied to cost per lead or cost per booking, not vanity volume. The challenge is delivering that promise consistently across a mixed client book with one account manager. Three things decide whether it holds.

1. The client book spans very different account types

High impact

Your ADS portfolio includes an eBike retailer and an electric vehicle dealer, an AV installation company, a radon testing service, a waste management firm, a leadership consultancy, a youth athletics program and an AI renovation app. Each needs a different campaign type, conversion goal and success metric. Without a fixed structure per track, account quality ends up depending on whoever touched it last.

2. Bidding is only as good as the conversion signal

High impact

Your program promises tracking verified before launch. In practice, mixed client accounts often count page views, button clicks and short calls as primary conversions. Smart Bidding and Meta then optimise toward those cheap signals, and reported CPL looks good while real leads stay flat.

Weak signal

  • Every click and page view set as primary
  • Calls counted at any duration
  • Meta pixel only, no Conversions API
  • No offline or CRM feedback

Clean signal

  • Only qualified forms, calls and purchases primary
  • Calls counted after a minimum duration
  • Meta CAPI with deduplication
  • Booked or closed leads sent back from the CRM

3. Reports need to explain, not just list numbers

Medium impact

Clients keep a retainer when they understand what changed and why. A report that leads with spend, leads, cost per lead or ROAS, then the two or three changes made and the next test, keeps the conversation about outcomes.
