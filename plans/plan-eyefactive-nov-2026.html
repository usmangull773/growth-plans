01

What we found

Search has worked for you for years, and it still can. What changed is who is still clicking. Questions like "what is interactive digital signage" are now answered inside AI Overviews and ChatGPT, so fewer people reach your site, but the ones who do are closer to buying. The setup has to follow that shift. These findings come from your public site and your job post. Each one gets confirmed or dropped in the week one account review.

1. Bidding probably learns from the wrong finish line

High impact

With a 3 to 18 month cycle, Google never sees the deal close. So Smart Bidding treats any contact form or demo request as the win. A student project, a one day trade fair rental and a museum tender all look equal, and the algorithm drifts toward the cheapest of them.

Root cause
Primary conversions stop at the website form. Nothing from the CRM flows back, so there is no value and no quality signal.
Fix
Import CRM stages as offline conversions with values. Make qualified lead and opportunity the primary goals. Keep downloads and newsletter sign ups as secondary only.

2. One site, many verticals, broad category keywords

High impact

The site targets retail, retail media, museums, hotels, trade fairs, DOOH and more, and sells hardware, rentals and the AppSuite subscription. Broad terms like "multitouch table" bring in buyers, home users, DIY builders and job seekers on the same budget. On a tight budget, that mix sets the cost per qualified lead.

Root cause
Campaigns are likely grouped by product rather than by vertical and intent, so budget cannot follow the segments that close.
Fix
Split Search by vertical and intent, give each its own budget and target, and move informational terms out of paid into content and AI search.

The 30 day plan

Fix the signal first, then the structure, then expand. Nothing new launches until the Google account is measuring the right thing.

Week by week

1

Review the Google account before touching bids

Map every conversion action and its status, then read 12 months of search terms split by vertical. Check how Search, Display, Video and Shopping each feed qualified leads, not just form fills.

Why: you keep what is working and only change what the data says is weak.
2

Feed CRM stages back as offline conversions

Send qualified lead, opportunity and won stages back to Google with values, using GCLID or enhanced conversions for leads. Downloads and newsletter sign ups move to secondary.

Why: with long cycles, bidding needs a signal that predicts revenue, not just activity.
3

Restructure Search by vertical and intent

Separate campaigns for retail and retail media, museums, hotels, AppSuite software and rentals. Shared negative lists for consumer, DIY, used and jobs terms. Each vertical lands on its closest case study.

Why: a tight budget should sit with the verticals that close, not the ones that click.
4

Launch Microsoft Ads at a small scale

Import the two or three strongest Google Search campaigns into Microsoft Ads for DACH and your English markets. Add LinkedIn profile bid adjustments for industry and job function, and check the segment report weekly.

Why: a second search engine with the same intent, usually cheaper clicks and a B2B audience layer Google does not have.
5

Decide on Display versus Demand Gen, then report

Test Demand Gen on site visitors and customer lists, using your Philips and Three Ireland case study videos. Judge it on assisted conversions and engaged views. Week four ends with a report and a brief for testing Meta, LinkedIn and Reddit.

Why: upper funnel spend is only worth keeping if it shows up later in the pipeline.
