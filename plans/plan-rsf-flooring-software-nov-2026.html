01

What we found

RSF is a new product with no ad history, a narrow buyer, and a sales funnel that ends on a demo call, not a checkout. The risk in the first 30 days is not low clicks. It is Meta optimising toward cheap form fills that never turn into held demos, so the test ends with spend and no answer.

High impact

1. Meta will chase the cheapest event unless the funnel reports back

A brand new pixel has no data. Demo bookings at a test budget will sit far below the roughly 50 optimisation events per ad set per week Meta wants to exit the learning phase. Left alone, the algorithm optimises toward whoever fills a form fastest, which in B2B is often students, employees and tyre kickers. The fix is passing every later stage back to Meta through the Conversions API so the campaign learns who actually shows up and pays.

High impact

2. Flooring owners are a narrow audience, so the creative has to do the targeting

Meta has no reliable "flooring business owner" job title filter. Interest stacks around flooring trade brands, associations and trade media help, but they leak into homeowners shopping for floors. The first line of every ad has to filter out homeowners and speak only to people who run crews, quotes and installs.

Pulls the wrong click

"Beautiful floors, managed better." Homeowners and designers click, demos fill with non buyers.

Pulls the owner

"Run a flooring company? Quotes, installs and crew schedules in one place, built by people who run one." Only owners recognise themselves.
Medium impact

3. Demos booked and demos held are different numbers

Booked demos look good in a report. Held demos are what pay. Without reminders and a short qualifying step, no show rates on cold B2B demos can eat a large share of the budget. The plan builds the qualifier and reminder flow before spend starts, and reports both numbers separately.

1

Build the stage tracking before a dollar is spent

Pixel plus Conversions API with five events: Lead, Qualified Lead, Demo Booked, Demo Held, Subscribed. Booked, held and paid are sent from your CRM or booking tool, with UTMs on every ad so each subscriber traces back to an ad.

Why: the scale or pause decision on day 30 is only as good as the stage data behind it.
2

Test three angles in week one

  • Built by a flooring owner (first angle). "Built inside a working flooring company, not by a software firm." Founder face, real shop, the exact headaches of quoting, scheduling and chasing installs.
  • Time back. The evening hours owners lose to quotes, measures and job paperwork, and what RSF takes off their plate.
  • Leaking profit. Missed follow ups, unbilled change orders and jobs that slip between sales and install.
Why: your static ads become the base, and a 30 to 45 second founder video usually carries trust better than design for a new B2B brand.
3

Qualify every demo request

Higher intent lead form or a short landing page with the five questions above, an instant confirmation, then reminders at 24 hours and 1 hour. Non qualified leads get a recorded product tour instead of a sales slot.

Why: protects your sales team's calendar and lifts the held rate, which is the number that drives cost per subscriber.
4

Run a small Google Search intent test

About $750 of the budget on high intent terms only: flooring business software, flooring estimating software, flooring CRM, and alternatives to established flooring systems such as RFMS and QFloors. Exact and phrase match, tight negatives.

Why: these searchers already want a tool, so this gives a cost per held demo benchmark to compare Meta against.
5

Move budget on held demos, then make the call

From week three, budget moves to the angle and channel with the lowest cost per held demo. Retargeting runs to video viewers and site visitors. Day 30 report covers spend, cost per stage, what was learned, what was not tracked, and a clear recommendation.

Why: you asked for a decision, not a dashboard.

Day 30 decision rules, targets set with you against your price and margin

Scale

Cost per held demo at or under target, held rate healthy, at least one paid subscriber traced to ads. Raise budget 20 to 30% a week.

Adjust

Leads qualify but held rate or close rate lags. Fix the reminder flow, offer or sales step before adding spend.

Pause

Cost per qualified lead far above target across all three angles after two weeks. Rework positioning before spending more.
