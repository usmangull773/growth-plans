What we found

Final expense buyers are mostly 50 to 85. Since January 2025, Meta files every US insurance ad under the Financial Products and Services category. That category removes age, ZIP code and lookalike targeting, so the old way of reaching seniors no longer works. Ads that skip the category get rejected or paused, and repeat problems lead to account restrictions.

The points below come from the job brief, your hiring history and Meta's published category rules. Each one gets confirmed in the week 1 account review.

Impact: High

Targeting has moved from the audience to the ad itself

When you cannot pick age 50 plus, the creative and the lead form have to do the filtering. An ad that says "life insurance" pulls in 25 year olds. An ad that speaks to seniors in the first second and asks a qualifying question on the form pulls in buyers.

What stopped workingAge 50 to 85 targeting, ZIP code lists, lookalikes of past buyers, interest stacks like "AARP" or "retirement"
What works inside the rulesSenior focused hooks and visuals, state level geo where agents are licensed, form questions on age and coverage amount, customer list audiences
Impact: High

Account stability depends on compliance, not on account age

Aged ad accounts bought from third parties carry someone else's history and can be shut down together with the Business Manager they sit in. A stable spend of $300 a day or more comes from a verified Business Manager, a correctly declared category and ads that pass review.

Impact: Medium

Meta optimises for form fills, not for policies sold

Leads land in GoHighLevel, Closebot follows up and agents work the phones. Meta never hears which leads picked up, booked or bought, so it keeps finding cheap form fillers. Sending the sales outcome back to Meta changes who it shows the ads to.

1

Account, policy and tracking audit

Review the Business Manager, verification status, rejected ads, category settings, pixel, Conversions API and the lead path into GoHighLevel. You get a written list of what is blocking delivery.

Why: you cannot scale spend on accounts that keep getting paused.

2

Rebuild campaigns inside the Financial Products rules

Fewer, larger ad sets by licensed state. Instant forms with higher intent settings and qualifying questions on age range, coverage amount and best time to call.

Why: broad delivery needs bigger ad sets to exit learning, and the form does the filtering the audience can no longer do.

3

Creative that selects seniors on its own

Three angles tested side by side: protecting family from funeral costs, fixed premiums that never rise, and coverage without a medical exam. Each one is written to pass insurance ad review.

Why: creative is now the targeting.

4

Zapier automations from lead to agent

New lead to GoHighLevel contact with source tags, Closebot text within seconds, agent alert, and a row in your sales leaderboard. Dispositions such as contacted, appointment set and policy sold get written back automatically.

Why: final expense leads go cold fast, and manual copying loses them.

5

Send sales outcomes back to Meta

Pass "appointment set" and "policy sold" from GoHighLevel to Meta through the Conversions API, then move optimisation toward quality leads.

Why: Meta learns who buys, not just who fills in a form.
