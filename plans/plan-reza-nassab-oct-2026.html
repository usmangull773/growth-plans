1

One price story across both sites

Agree which site owns each surgical procedure, then align the "from" price and consultation fee, or explain the difference in one line (surgeon, implant, location). Cross links between the two sites point to the page that owns the enquiry.

Why: a patient who sees two prices for one surgeon hesitates, and hesitation at £8,500 costs a consultation.
2

Measure every route to a booked consultation

Forms on both sites, calls to 0800 0584558 and WhatsApp each recorded as a conversion with its source, then consultation booked and surgery booked imported as offline conversions.

Why: bidding should learn from patients who book surgery, not from form fills alone.
3

Search coverage by procedure

Breast augmentation, rhinoplasty, deep plane facelift, mummy makeover and tummy tuck each get their own ad group, ad copy and cost per consultation target, sent to the procedure page that already carries price, finance and a form. Your name and CLNQ are protected on brand searches.

Why: these are four and five figure procedures, and the right search is worth paying for.
4

A faster step from enquiry to consultation

Add an evening call back option to match your Tuesday and Thursday hours, an instant confirmation to every enquiry, consultation reminders, and a polite follow-up for quotes not yet booked once the cooling-off period has passed.

Why: the cheapest new patient is the enquiry you already paid for.
5

Homepage fixes and one weekly report

Location and specialty added to the homepage title and H1. Then one report each week: enquiries, consultations booked, attended and surgery booked, by procedure and by site. Where a number cannot be measured yet, the report says so.

Why: you get decisions, not a longer dashboard.
