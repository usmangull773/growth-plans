01

What we found

Your post describes a strong setup with one missing piece. The media buyer runs the campaigns and the creative team builds the assets, but nobody currently owns the link between the numbers and the next round of creative. That link is the role. Three things follow from it.

1. Performance data and creative work run on separate tracks

High impact

When results come back from the media buyer, they usually describe what happened on each platform. They rarely say what the creative team should make next. Without someone translating, new creative gets built from taste and the brief from the last meeting, not from which hooks, offers and formats actually won.

2. Briefs need a fixed structure that starts with a data point

High impact

A creative brief that opens with "make something for the holidays" produces assets that are hard to judge. A brief that opens with one proven insight and one hypothesis to test gives the creative team a clear target and gives the media buyer a clean test to run.

AreaWithout a strategy ownerWith this plan

Client meetingsRecap of numbersThree decisions, each backed by one chart

3. The US retail calendar needs planning six to eight weeks ahead

Medium impact

Holiday creative is already locked for most brands by now. The opportunity is to run the remaining peak days cleanly and have Q1 and Q2 briefs ready before January, so the creative team is never building under deadline.

Suggested split of the 10 weekly hours.

1
Week 1

Onboarding and a 90 day data review

Read the media buyer's last 90 days of Meta and Google reports, Shopify sales, and the past creative library. Tag each ad by hook, offer and format.

Why: you cannot brief the next ad until you know which past ads actually sold.

2
Week 1 onward

One weekly scorecard everyone reads

A single page: Shopify revenue against total ad spend, new versus returning customers, and the top and bottom three creatives on each platform.

Why: Meta and Google can both claim the same sale. A blended view keeps decisions honest.

3
Weeks 2 to 3

Creative brief template and the first two briefs

A one page format: insight, audience, hook, offer, format, KPI to beat. First two briefs cover the December gifting window and January acquisition.

Why: the creative team gets a clear target and the media buyer gets a clean test.

4
Weeks 3 to 4

Q1 and Q2 seasonal calendar

Key US retail moments through Mother's Day, each with a brief due date, creative deadline and budget note for the media buyer.

Why: briefs land six to eight weeks ahead, so nothing is rushed.

5
Week 4

First monthly strategy review with the client

Three recommendations, each with one supporting chart, plus the test log showing what was learned and what goes into next month's briefs.

Why: the client sees decisions, not a recap of numbers they already have.
