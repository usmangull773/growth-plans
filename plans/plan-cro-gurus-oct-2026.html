01

What we found

Your brief describes an account with campaigns live, spend going out, and a sense that results are capped below what the account can do. In almost every account like this, the cap comes from three places. Each one is checked in the first week, and each has a clear fix.

1. Bidding may be optimizing toward the wrong signal

High impact

Smart Bidding is only as good as the conversion it is told to chase. When page views, add to carts or form starts are set as primary conversions, Google finds more of those, not more customers. This is the first thing we verify, because every other change depends on it.

What holds performance back
  • Micro actions counted as primary conversions
  • Duplicate tags inflating results
  • No offline or backend match, so ROAS is a guess
What works
  • One primary action per goal: purchase or qualified lead
  • Google Ads, GA4 and backend numbers reconciled
  • Micro actions kept as secondary, for insight only

2. Wasted spend usually sits in three predictable places

High impact

You asked for better keyword targeting and less waste. Waste is rarely spread evenly. It concentrates in a short list of search terms and placements, and cutting those frees budget for the campaigns that already work.

Leak pointWhere we lookThe fix
Irrelevant search terms90 day search term report, broad and phrase matchShared negative lists, match type tightening
Low intent placementsPerformance Max and Display placement reportsAccount level exclusions, brand and placement controls
Ad to page mismatchAd groups with high CTR but low conversion rateRewritten ads and closer landing page match

3. Scaling needs a rule, not a guess

Medium impact

Raising budgets too fast resets learning and pushes cost per conversion up. Scaling works when it follows a clear threshold, so only proven campaigns get more money.

Campaign statusAction
Holding target CPA or ROAS for 7 daysRaise budget 15 to 20 percent, then hold
Close to targetTest ads and bid strategy before adding budget
Well off target after cleanupRestructure or move budget to stronger campaigns

The 30 day plan

Five steps, in an order where each one makes the next more effective. You get a short written update at the end of every week, with what changed, why, and what is next.

1

Full account and tracking audit

Review structure, conversion actions, attribution, GA4 and GTM setup, and, for eCommerce, the Merchant Center feed. Fix anything that sends bidding the wrong signal.

Why: every later decision relies on numbers you can trust.
2

Cut wasted spend and tighten targeting

Search term mining, negative keyword lists, match type review, placement exclusions, and budget moved away from terms that spend without converting.

Why: the fastest gain usually comes from stopping spend that never converts.
3

Test new ads and bid strategies

New responsive search ads built around the strongest offers, plus a controlled bid strategy test with a set pass or fail target agreed with you up front.

Why: tests with a clear target tell you what to keep, not just what changed.
4

Scale what is profitable

Campaigns that hold target CPA or ROAS get staged budget increases. Weak campaigns get restructured or their budget moves to stronger ones.

Why: growth only counts if cost per result holds as spend rises.
5

Clear reporting and a next 60 day roadmap

A written summary of every change made, the result, and a prioritized list of the next opportunities, so the account can keep improving without constant direction.

Why: you asked for someone who takes initiative and keeps you informed.
