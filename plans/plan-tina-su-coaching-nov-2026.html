01

What we found

From the outside, the offer is strong: real parent stories, a founder story that moves people, and a free live training that sells a $2,500 program. The risk is not the offer. It is that one ad message is carrying a five figure monthly budget, and the data coming back is too thin to show which ad actually fills each launch.

High impact

One message is doing all the work

All six live ads open with the same line, "You've heard it before", point to the same headline, "Free Live Training: Speech Gains in 90 Days", and land on the same registration page. At this spend, the same audience sees the same hook again and again, and cost per registration tends to creep up week after week. Copy that speaks directly about a child's diagnosis can also attract extra review under Meta's personal attributes rules, so a compliant backup angle should always be ready.

High impact

The ad to enrollment trail is hard to follow

Every ad link carries only utm_source=facebook. The registration page loads the Meta pixel and fires a PageView, but it does not run the Tag Manager container that the speech.tinasu.com pages use. So when a parent registers, shows up live and later buys Hi Mama, there is no clean way to say which ad, angle or audience earned that sale. Meta is left optimising toward registrations, not toward parents who attend and enroll.

Now

  • One UTM value for every ad
  • PageView on the registration page
  • Registrations judged in Ads Manager only
  • No link from ad to show-up or purchase

After week one

  • UTMs by campaign, ad set and ad
  • Lead, attended and purchase events, with Conversions API and deduplication
  • Cost per registration, per attendee and per enrollment in one weekly view
  • Budget follows the ads that produce buyers
Medium impact

Four domains, one parent journey

A parent can move across four domains before buying. That is fine, but each hop needs the same pixel, the same first party data and consistent dates. One example: the Reverse Picky Eating waitlist page still shows the October 2025 enrollment dates, which can make a warm parent hesitate at the last step.

1

Learn the parents before touching the account

Watch the live training and the Mom to Mom replays, read the FAQ answers and DMs, and list the exact words parents use: "non-verbal", "echolalia", "tried everything", "I want mama".

Why: ads that sound like a parent beat ads that sound like an advertiser.
2

Fix the trail from ad to enrollment

Ad level UTMs, Lead and attendance events, purchase events from the checkout, Conversions API with deduplication, and a single weekly sheet showing cost per registration, show-up rate and cost per enrollment.

Why: budget should follow buyers, not just sign-ups.
3

Build three new angles, finished and ready to run

Parent story cuts from the replays, Tina's own story as a short video, and objection ads ("Is my child too old?", "Do I need my partner on board?"). Each angle as static images and short video edits from footage you provide, with a compliant version of every diagnosis-led line.

Why: fresh hooks keep cost per registration steady at high spend.
4

Test with a clear rule, then scale

A dedicated creative test budget, one variable per test, judged on cost per attendee rather than clicks. Winners move into the main campaign, losers are switched off without debate.

Why: you get a steady list of what to make next.
5

Write the launch pacing playbook

Budget curves for the weeks before a training, retargeting for registrants who missed it live, replay viewers and waitlist parents, and a short checklist for weekend checks during enrollment.

Why: launches are where the year's revenue is made, so nothing should be decided on the day.
