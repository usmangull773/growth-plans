01 What we found

The constraint is measurement and margin, not reach

This review used only what is public: the storefront, its tags, its product catalogue, its markets and its team page. From the outside, Aavalabs looks like a brand with strong products, strong marketplace specialists and a wide European footprint. What is missing is a single view of what each channel adds to profit. Without it, budget follows platform ROAS, discounts hide margin loss, and nobody can say with confidence what the next €10,000 should buy.

High impact

Tracking covers Google and Meta, but cannot yet prove incrementality

Tags load through native Shopify apps, not a managed tag container. Google Ads, GA4, Merchant Center, the Meta pixel and Klaviyo all fire. No Microsoft UET tag was found on the homepage or product pages, so Microsoft Ads is likely optimising on imported or partial data. Each platform also claims its own sales, so the same order can be counted two or three times across channels.

High impact

Order economics put pressure on margin, and discounts make ROAS look better than it is

Single products sit mostly between €18.99 and €35.99, and Black Week runs 25% off everything and 30% off bundles. A Lion's Mane order at €17.25 rarely covers a paid first order on its own. Profit has to come from the second, third and fourth order, but no subscribe and save option was found on any product, so repeat purchase depends on customers coming back by themselves.

Running now

  • Budget judged on platform ROAS per channel
  • Discount led first orders at €17 to €26
  • Repeat orders left to email and memory
  • Same ROAS target for new and returning buyers

What scales

  • Budget judged on blended MER and new customer CAC
  • Bundles and 90 day packs as the default first order
  • Subscribe and save on the 10 best repeat products
  • Higher bids for new customers, lower for existing ones
Medium impact

Markets and claims are not ready for more spend everywhere

Paid traffic in Poland, Sweden, Denmark and Finland lands on English pages, while Germany, France, Italy, Spain and the Netherlands get native language pages. On claims, most product copy is careful, but a few titles and meta descriptions attach a benefit to a botanical rather than to an authorised nutrient. These are the lines that Google Shopping and ads pull straight into listings.

1

Audit every account and agree one baseline

Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Allegro and Shop Apotheke, plus GA4 and Shopify. Reconcile platform-reported sales against Shopify orders by market. Agree the baseline with leadership: blended MER, new customer CAC, contribution margin per order and payback period.

Reason: every later decision needs one number everyone trusts, not six platform dashboards.

2

Find wasted spend and price it in euros

Search terms with no sales, brand traffic paid for twice in Search and Performance Max, low margin products eating Shopping budget, existing customers bought again as "new", marketplace ads judged on ACoS instead of TACoS. Each item gets a € estimate and an owner.

Reason: a list ranked by € impact tells the team what to fix first.

3

Fix tracking and attribution

Consent Mode v2 checked for EU visitors, Enhanced Conversions, Meta CAPI deduplication, Microsoft UET with consent, and server-side routing where it adds accuracy. Then turn on new customer acquisition goals in Google and Meta, using margin-based values instead of revenue.

Reason: bidding can only chase profitable new customers if the signal tells it who they are.

4

Design the first incrementality tests

A geo holdout on Meta in one matched market, a brand search holdout, and a Performance Max versus Shopping split. Each test gets a written hypothesis, duration, budget and decision rule before launch.

Reason: tests show which channels truly drive sales, and they feed a light media mix model once 6 to 12 months of clean data exist.

5

Build the 12 month roadmap and train the team

A budget split by channel and market with three spend scenarios, a claims checklist for every ad, and one playbook per role. Weekly training sessions so the team runs day to day optimisation without outside help.

Reason: the goal by month 6 is a team that runs it alone, with strategy reviewed monthly.
