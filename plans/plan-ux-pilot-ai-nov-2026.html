01

What we found

Organic search, founder content and a 600K newsletter took UX Pilot to $5.3M ARR. Paid search has not matched that, and the job post says why: you want to know which past campaigns brought paying users, and you want purchases to flow back into Google and Microsoft. That points to three root causes.

1. Bidding is optimising toward free signups, not purchases

High impact

UX Pilot has a free plan with no credit card. If the primary conversion in Google Ads and Microsoft Ads is a signup, Smart Bidding finds the cheapest people who will create a free account. Those are often students, hobbyists and users outside your buyer countries. The algorithm is doing its job, pointed at the wrong goal. The paid event happens later in Stripe or your billing system, so the ad platforms never see it.

How it usually runs

  • Primary conversion: signup or trial start
  • Purchase happens days later, never sent back
  • Click ID not stored against the user
  • Reports show cost per signup, not cost per customer

How it should run

  • Click ID (gclid, msclkid) saved on the user record at signup
  • First payment sent back as an offline conversion with value
  • Signup kept as a secondary, observe-only action
  • Bidding on purchase value, reported as cost per paying customer

2. Geo and network settings are leaking budget outside the 8 countries

High impact

Both platforms default to targeting people "in or interested in" a location. On a global SaaS product that means a large share of clicks can come from outside your buyer countries. Performance Max, Search Partners and the Microsoft Audience Network widen the leak further. These are the first settings to check.

3. Ads are not matched to the high intent pages that already convert organically

Medium impact

uxpilot.ai already has dedicated pages for tools and competitor comparisons, and those pages rank. Paid search should mirror what SEO has proven: one intent, one ad group, one matching landing page, instead of sending every click to the homepage.

1

Audit every past Google and Microsoft campaign against paying customers

Pull full history through the Google Ads API and Microsoft Ads API, then join campaign, keyword and search term data to your paid customer list by click ID or signup date and country.

Why: you get a ranked list of what actually produced revenue, and what only produced free accounts.
2

Send purchases back to both platforms

Store gclid, gbraid, wbraid and msclkid on the user at signup. Upload first payment and plan value as offline conversions (Google enhanced conversions for leads, Microsoft offline conversion import), daily and automated.

Why: Smart Bidding can only find buyers once it sees who bought.
3

Lock geo, language and networks to the 8 countries

Presence only targeting, explicit exclusions, Search Partners and Audience Network off at relaunch, PMax held back until purchase data is flowing.

Why: every click outside your buyer markets is a cost with no chance of a sale.
4

Relaunch Search around proven intent, with matched landing pages

Separate campaigns for brand, high intent generators, and competitor alternatives. Each ad group points to its matching page. Bidding starts on Maximise Conversions with purchase as the only primary action, then moves to target CPA or ROAS once volume allows.

Why: matched pages lift conversion rate and Quality Score, which lowers cost per customer.
5

Run the agent loops and a short weekly update

Daily search term mining and negative keyword suggestions, ad copy variants for testing, landing page match checks, budget pacing alerts, and a one screen weekly report on spend, paying customers and cost per paying customer by country.

Why: the heavy lifting runs every day without adding hours, and you read one short update a week.
