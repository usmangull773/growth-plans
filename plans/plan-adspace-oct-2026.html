01 · What we found

What a senior operator on your team has to solve

Your own DTC Meta guidance says the job is to acquire valuable customers at an economically sustainable cost, not to maximise platform ROAS in isolation. Three things decide whether an account manager delivers that or just reports it.

1. Platform ROAS and store revenue tell two different stories

High impact

Meta and Google both claim credit for the same orders, and returning customers make acquisition look stronger than it is. If budget follows platform ROAS, spend drifts toward people who were going to buy anyway. The fix is to steer on new-customer CAC and blended MER from Shopify data, with platform numbers used for diagnosis.

Steering on platform viewSteering on business view
Meta ROAS plus Google ROAS adds up to more revenue than the store bookedBlended MER: total net revenue divided by total paid spend
Retargeting and brand search look like the best campaignsNew-customer CAC from first-time Shopify orders, reported per channel
Budget scaled on a good 3 day ROAS windowBudget scaled only when marginal CAC holds under the agreed ceiling
Client asks why revenue did not move with ROASOne metric dictionary, so client, Head of Paid Media and buyer read the same number

2. Fragmented structures stop new accounts from learning

High impact

Brands that arrive with many small ad sets and overlapping campaigns split conversion signal so thin that nothing exits learning. Consolidating into three clear lanes per platform gives the algorithms enough volume and gives the client a structure they can understand in one slide.

Typical inherited account
12 to 20 ad sets, each under 50 purchases a week
Interest stacks overlapping each other
Retargeting and prospecting sharing budget
One PMax with every SKU, no margin split

3. Clients judge the manager by the weekly readout

Medium impact

The role presents results directly to clients. A good readout answers four questions in under five minutes, and ties every recommendation back to CAC, MER and contribution margin. It plugs into whatever reporting stack Adspace already runs.

1

Days 1 to 7: audit, tracking and the metric dictionary

Why: no budget decision is safe until the numbers agree.
  • Check Meta pixel and Conversions API dedup, event match quality, and the Shopify purchase value sent to both platforms.
  • Check Google Ads conversion source (Shopify or GA4, never both as primary), enhanced conversions and Merchant Center feed health.
  • Agree definitions with the Head of Paid Media: net revenue, first-time customer, CAC spend, margin, attribution windows.
  • Set the 30 day baseline: MER, new-customer CAC, AOV, and the CAC ceiling the brand can afford.

3

Days 8 to 30: creative testing with a written log

Why: creative is the biggest lever on Meta, and every test should leave a lesson.
  • Test concepts before variations: new angle, persona or offer first, then hooks, then formats.
  • Each test logs the hypothesis, the variable, spend, the success metric chosen before launch, and the result.
  • Kill rule: an ad that spends 1.5x the CAC ceiling with no purchase is paused. Graduate rule: 3 days at or under target CAC moves it to Scale.
  • Fatigue is read across frequency, CTR trend, hook rate and CPA together, never frequency alone. The creative team gets a weekly brief on what to make next.
4

Days 15 to 30: scale, hold, reduce rules

Why: rules keep CAC stable while spend grows, and make every change easy to defend to the client.
  • Scale: raise budget 15 to 20% every 72 hours while 3 day new-customer CAC stays under the ceiling and MER holds.
  • Hold: CAC within 10% over target, or the campaign is still in learning.
  • Reduce: MER below the floor for 2 days in a row, or CAC 20%+ over the ceiling.
  • Test: 15 to 20% of spend always goes to new creative and new Google surfaces.
5

Day 30: what success looks like

Why: the client should be able to judge the first month in one page.
  • Tracking verified, and Shopify, Meta and Google revenue reconciled within an agreed tolerance.
  • New-customer CAC at or under the agreed ceiling, with MER holding at or above baseline.
  • At least 2 winning concepts moved into Scale, and a 30 day test log the creative team can work from.
  • A month two plan with a spend target and the rules that will govern it.
