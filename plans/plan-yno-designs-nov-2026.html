01

What we found

Your proof is already enterprise grade: work for teams at Morgan Stanley, BMO, Broadridge, Verisk and NYC OTI, plus measured outcomes like a 39% productivity gain and 9 minutes saved per HR case. The gap is not credibility. The gap is that the funnel cannot tell a $20K+ buyer from a $999 one, so any ad campaign would optimize toward whoever fills the form fastest.

1. Your most valuable action is invisible to ads

High impact

Every "Get a Free Consultation" and "Book Time" link on ynodesigns.com goes to an external calendar booking page. Your site already runs Google Tag Manager, but a tag cannot fire on a page you do not control. So Google would see the click, never the booked call, and would learn nothing about which searches produce real buyers.

Ad clickthenynodesigns.comthen"Free Consultation"thenExternal calendar: no tracking
Ad clickthenOffer pagethenQualifier step: trackedthenCalendar for qualified leads onlythenQualified call sent back to Google

2. The form cannot qualify a $20K+ project

High impact

The current form collects name, email, phone and message. That is fine for organic inquiries, but paid traffic needs a filter so your team only spends discovery time on the right buyers, and so Google can be trained on the leads that matter.

3. Public signals pull the price down

Medium impact

Your directory listing shows a $5,000 minimum project and packages from $999, and the site has location pages built for small local markets. Those are useful for organic reach, but paid traffic should land on pages that speak only to enterprise problems and show enterprise proof.

Sends the wrong signal to ad trafficPackages from $999. $5,000 minimum project. City pages (Paterson, West Milford, Lakewood). Homepage title led by "AI, Web 3.0 and blockchain".
What ad traffic should seeOne page per enterprise problem. Client logos and your measured outcomes above the fold. Budget band in the form. A clear "UX first, development optional" engagement model.

1

Rebuild tracking around qualified leads (week 1)

So Google learns from $20K+ buyers, not from anyone who clicks a button.

  • Add the qualifier step before the calendar, inside your existing Tag Manager container.
  • Fire separate conversions for "lead" and "qualified lead" (budget $20K+ and company 50+).
  • Import booked and held discovery calls back into Google Ads as offline conversions.
2

Three offer pages, one per enterprise problem (weeks 1 to 2)

A buyer searching for legacy modernization should land on legacy modernization, not a general homepage.

  • Legacy system modernization: "From 3 systems to 1, 37% less manual work".
  • Custom CRM and internal tools: "9 minutes saved per HR case".
  • AI-enabled workflows and dashboards: "39% boost in productivity".
3

Google Search on problem-led, high-intent terms (from week 2)

People searching for a fix to an internal tool are closer to a budget than people searching "UX agency".

4

Microsoft Ads with professional profile targeting (from week 2)

Puts the same offer in front of decision makers by company, industry and job function, on search intent.

  • Industries: financial services, insurance, healthcare, government.
  • Job functions: operations, IT, product, HR systems.
  • Bid up on companies with 200+ employees.
5

Optimize weekly and report on qualified leads (weeks 3 to 4)

One number matters: cost per qualified discovery call.

  • Search term clean-up twice a week, budget moved to the cheapest qualified source.
  • Weekly one-page report: spend, leads, qualified leads, calls booked, cost per qualified call.
  • Switch bidding to qualified conversions once volume allows.
