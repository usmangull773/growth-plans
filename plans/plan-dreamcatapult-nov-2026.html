01

What we found

Your brief asks for one person to own campaigns and tracking end to end. Looking at the brands you work with, the real challenge is that hospitality revenue is completed on third party booking tools, not on the client's own website, so ads optimise on whatever signal is left over. These are the gaps we would close first.

Bookings finish on systems that Google and Meta cannot see by default

High impact

On a boutique hotel site in your portfolio, the "Book now" menu opens six different journeys: rooms, restaurant tables, bar tables, spa appointments, event enquiries and gift cards. Each one completes inside a different tool. Unless each tool passes its result back, Google Ads and Meta only see a click on "Book now", not the booking or its value.

#Booking pathConversion it should send
1Room reservationPurchase with revenue and currency, from the booking engine's GA4 and GTM integration, with cross domain linking so the original ad click is kept.
2Restaurant and bar tablesReservation event with covers and venue, fired on the widget's confirmation step, imported to Google Ads as a primary conversion.
3Spa appointmentsBooking event with treatment value where the tool exposes it.
4Events and weddingsQualified lead from the quote form, with a value assigned so bidding favours larger enquiries.
5Gift cardsPurchase with value, kept apart from room revenue so reports stay clean.
6Meta for every pathSame events through Pixel and Conversions API with a shared event ID, so Meta counts each booking once.

Some client sites send no measurable signal at all

High impact

On first load of a Michelin starred restaurant site in your portfolio, we saw no Google tag, no GA4 and no Meta Pixel, and its table booking runs through an external reservation page. Tags may be held back until consent, which is correct in the EU, but then Consent Mode v2 has to be in place or paid campaigns lose most of their conversion data.

Without itWith it
Tag setupNo container, or tags hard coded in the themeOne GTM container per client, named versions, one owner
ConsentTags blocked with no modelled data, or firing before consentConsent Mode v2 with a certified banner, tested in GTM Preview
ReservationsCounted only inside the booking toolReservation event in GA4, Google Ads and Meta
BiddingOptimises toward clicks and page viewsOptimises toward real bookings

Merchant Center and launches need one repeatable process

Medium impact

With Shopping, Performance Max and Meta running for several clients at once, most problems come from small things: a price on the site that no longer matches the feed, missing shipping settings, or a campaign launched without a final check. One process removes most of them before they cost money.

Without a process
Feed or campaign changeNo checkDisapprovals and wasted spend
With a process
Feed or campaign changeChecklist and change logDiagnostics reviewedApproved and tracked

Every change is written in a shared change log with the date, account, what changed and why, so anyone on your team can see what happened in an account without asking.

1
Week 1

Tracking audit on every live client

  • GTM container, GA4 key events, Google Ads conversion actions and Meta Events Manager checked side by side.
  • Consent Mode v2 status, Meta event match quality and duplicate events recorded.
  • One audit sheet per client: what works, what is missing, what to fix first.
Why: optimising campaigns before the conversions are right only trains bidding on the wrong signal.
2
Weeks 1 and 2

Booking engine, reservation and purchase tracking

  • Purchase events with value from each booking engine and shop, with cross domain linking in GA4.
  • Reservation and enquiry events for tables, spa and events, tested with real test bookings.
  • Meta Pixel plus Conversions API with a shared event ID, and enhanced conversions in Google Ads.
Why: Smart Bidding and Meta's delivery only scale what they can count.
3
Week 2

Merchant Center clean up

  • Diagnostics reviewed, price and availability mismatches traced to the source, shipping and tax settings corrected.
  • Feed rules and supplemental feeds for titles, product types and custom labels, then products sent for re-review.
Why: every disapproved product is a product your Shopping and Performance Max campaigns cannot sell.
4
Weeks 2 and 3

Pre-launch checklist and change log

  • Written checklist for every new Google and Meta campaign: conversions, budgets, locations, languages, schedules, negatives, URLs, assets and policy.
  • Shared change log for every account, updated the same day as the change.
Why: launch mistakes are cheap to catch before go live and expensive after.
5
Weeks 3 and 4

Optimise, then report every week

  • Budget pacing, search terms, audiences and creative tests run on real booking data.
  • One weekly report per client in Looker Studio on the same day each week, with tasks updated in your tool.
Why: you and your clients see the same numbers, and nobody has to chase for status.
